### Selected problem

MSU-IIT students, staff, and visitors spend time wandering across college buildings searching for different offices because indoor floor plans are not mapped in standard navigation maps, leading to unnecessary delays when doing tasks.

### Selection rationale

We chose this as the problem to be solved because digitizing campus navigation solves a practical issue that many students, organization officers, and visitors who are unfamiliar with campus grounds often face when locating administrative desks and faculty offices across multiple campus buildings. We have easy access to stakeholders since we can interview students and office staff on campus. The canteen problem was not chosen because of the reliance on e-wallet integration and vendor cooperation, which are hard to implement within our timeline. Likewise, the smoke and haze dashboard idea was the most difficult of the three. It was considered too technical since handling high-volume data payloads and cleaning and normalizing them would be a massive bottleneck in development.

Our student survey (see "Survey Evidence" below) supports this choice. Among respondents who completed it, 72% (21 of 29) reported at least one problem the last time they looked for an unfamiliar office or room, and every respondent said they would use a campus directory.

### Survey Evidence

We ran a student survey, "Finding offices and rooms at MSU-IIT", to check whether the problem is real and what the directory should include. The tables below summarize the responses.

**Who answered.** We received 32 submissions. Two respondents did not consent and were excluded, which left 30 consenting respondents, all MSU-IIT students. One of them said they have never needed to find an unfamiliar place and stopped there, so 29 respondents completed the survey. They were 11 first-years, 6 second-years, 9 third-years, and 3 fourth-years or above. Ten were organization officers, 8 were members, and 11 were not in an organization. Colleges represented were COE (8), CASS (6), CCS (4), CEBA (4), CSM (2), and CED (2). Three respondents left the college question blank.

## 3\. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

**iitmaps**: every office, one search away 

### Problem statement

MSU-IIT students, especially freshmen and organization officers, along with visitors and researchers, often need to find offices, departments, and administrative desks spread across campus. Standard navigation apps map only outdoor roads, and posted floor plans are few or hard to read. In our survey of 29 students, 21 (72%) reported at least one problem the last time they looked for an unfamiliar place. Most asked someone (72%) or walked around (52%), 48% went to the wrong place first, and 48% felt frustrated or stressed. Among the 16 organization members and officers who run errands, 11 said finding offices took at least half of their time. We want a mobile-friendly digital directory with indoor floor plans that lets users search for an office and see where it is.

### Proposed solution

We propose a web-based interactive spatial directory for MSU-IIT that digitizes existing campus and floor maps into a searchable, mobile-friendly platform. Users may type the name of an office, department, or administrative desk, or a room number, and instantly see its building, floor, and room highlighted on the floor plan, along with its office hours. Users can also browse offices by building and floor, and report wrong or outdated information through a simple link. We will start with two pilot buildings, the Administrative Building and CASS, because they were among the most-mentioned destinations in our survey. We will build the plans from existing campus maps and verify room locations through on-site visits. The directory should be open to everyone without an account, so guests and visitors can use it. It should be light enough to load quickly on a phone, and we will try to keep it usable offline once loaded. We will survey and interview students, organization officers, office staff, and visitors to test whether the tool is helpful and preferred over seeking help. By replacing static posted floor plans and asking around for instant, searchable answers, the directory helps users reach the right office faster, especially when they have urgent tasks to do.

## 5\. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting

Students and other people who need directions at MSU-IIT are our intended users for this project. When they want to locate a building, office, or a particular room on campus for whatever purpose, our project will provide them with the information they need, saving time and energy compared to asking for directions and unclear wayfinding.

### In-scope capabilities

1. Search for a faculty office, department office, or administrative desk by name or by room number.  
2. View the resulting building, floor, and room highlighted on a digital floor plan, and switch between floors and buildings.  
3. Browse or filter offices by building and floor.  
4. Show office hours on each office listing.  
5. Let users report wrong or outdated information with a simple link.

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. Indoor map data other than the Administrative Building and CASS.  
2. Live indoor GPS tracking.  
3. Turn-by-turn navigation and routing between rooms.  
4. Native mobile apps and user accounts.  
5. Facility booking, room availability, and non-office points of interest such as trash bins.  
6. Staff directory with personnel names and photos (to be considered later).  

### Expected deliverables

1. A working prototype of iitmaps with search, floor plan view, floor and building switching, browse by building and floor, office hours, and a report link for the Administrative Building and CASS.  
2. A digitized dataset of floor plans and office listings for the Administrative Building and CASS, with a verified date for each entry.  
3. Project documentation, including this proposal, a short user guide, and setup notes for running the prototype.  
4. Testing evidence, including survey responses, interview notes, and usability test records.  
5. A source code repository with the final prototype.

### Constraints

| Constraint | Effect on the project | How the team will respond |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Limited time in one semester | Only a few buildings and core features can be built | Limit the scope to two pilot buildings and five core capabilities. Treat offline use as a stretch goal. |
| Room data must be collected and checked manually | Slow work, and listings may be wrong if offices have moved | Use existing campus maps as a base, verify through on-site visits, and record the date each listing was last checked |
| Limited access to official floor plans | The base layouts may be incomplete | Request plans from the administration, and sketch from on-site measurements if unavailable |
| Survey covered students only | Staff, administration, and visitor needs and concerns are not yet validated | Interview office staff and visitors, and request a meeting with the administration before building the dataset |
| Small convenience sample (29 completed responses) | Survey figures show direction but are not precise estimates for all of MSU-IIT | Report figures as results from our sample only, and repeat key questions during prototype testing |

### Assumptions and dependencies

| Assumption or dependency | Why it matters | How to check or address it |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Existing campus maps are accurate enough to serve as a starting layout | Inaccurate maps would mislead users and add rework | Compare the map against a sample of rooms on every floor of the Administrative Building and CASS during on site visits |
| Office locations must or stay stable during the semester | Outdated office listings would send users to the wrong location, which defeats the purpose of the directory | Ask office staff about planned moves and record a “last verified” date for each entry |
| Users will actually open a new site when they need to find a place | In the survey, 5 of the 21 respondents who gave a reason said they do not want to open another app or site, and 12 said they do not need to find places often | Keep the directory free of accounts and fast to load, share the link on posters and org channels, and measure real use during testing |
| The administration allows mapping of the Administrative Building | This building was the most-mentioned destination in the survey but may have restricted areas | Ask the administration which areas should not be mapped before collecting data |

## 6\. Review and Next Steps

### Changes made after reviewing the proposal

- Before review: The directory would cover offices across several colleges, including CCS, CASS, CSM, and COE.   
- After review: The first version covers only two pilot buildings, CASS and CCS. Other buildings are out of scope for this semester.   
- Reason: Mapping every college would require verifying many rooms within one semester. Two pilot buildings keep the data accurate and let us test the idea with real users before expanding. 

### Changes made after the student survey

- **Pilot buildings.** We replaced CCS with the Administrative Building, so the pilots are now the Administrative Building and CASS. In the survey, the Administrative Building was the most-mentioned destination (5 of 26 named buildings), CASS tied for second (4), and CCS received 2. These counts are small, so we will confirm the choice during interviews. CCS is now a stretch building if time allows.
- **Features.** The in-scope list now has five capabilities. We added search by room number (74% of 23 would use it), office hours (65%), and a report link (23 of 29 said they would report a wrong listing). Search by name (87%), browse by building and floor (83%), and the highlighted floor plan (74%) stay as they were.
- **Problem statement.** We added the survey evidence and trimmed it to stay within 100 to 150 words.
- **Solution.** We added guest access without accounts, a lightweight design, and a goal of offline use after the first load. These came from respondent requests (open to guests, offline access) and from the 5 respondents who did not want to open another app or site.
- **Stakeholder list.** We added a survey status column. Students and organization officers have been surveyed. Staff, administration, and visitors have not.
- **Out of scope.** We added native apps and accounts, facility booking and non-office points of interest, and a personnel directory. Respondents asked for these, but they do not fit one semester.
- **Success indicators.** We added baseline numbers from the survey so we can measure improvement.
- **Constraints and assumptions.** We added the student-only, small-sample limitation and the risk that users may not open a new site.

### Biggest remaining risk

Our dependence on accurate floor plan data. We do not yet know whether usable floor plans of the Administrative Building and CASS exist, and manual mapping may be slow or contain errors. Offices may also move, which would make listings wrong and lose users’ trust. If the data is unreliable, the directory could send people to the wrong room, which is worse than the current approach. The survey shows both sides of this risk: 23 of 29 respondents said they would report a wrong listing, 16 would double-check, and 1 would stop using the directory after one wrong room. We will reduce this by verifying a sample of rooms through on-site visits, recording a last-verified date for each listing, and making the report link easy to use.

### Unanswered questions

1. Can we obtain official or more detailed floor plans for the Administrative Building and CASS from the colleges or administration?  
2. Will users actually prefer the directory over asking someone for help, especially when they have urgent submissions? The survey shows strong intent (all 29 would use it, 15 instead of asking someone), but intent is not behavior, and 14 would still ask someone if unsure. We still need usability testing.  
3. How long will it take our team to collect and verify the room data for every floor of the Administrative Building and CASS, and can this be finished early enough to leave time for building and testing the prototype?  
4. What do office staff, administrators, and visitors think about listing offices (privacy, accuracy, security)? Our survey did not reach them.
